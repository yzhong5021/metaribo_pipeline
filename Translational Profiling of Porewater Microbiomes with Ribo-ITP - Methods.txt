Metagenomic preprocessing and assembly

Metagenomics data were processed primarily using the nf-core/mag pipeline. First, fastp (Chen et al. 2018) was used to trim adapters and filter out low-quality reads (--detect_adapter_for_pe -q 15 --cut_front --cut_tail --cut_mean_quality 15 --length_required 15 --trim_poly_g). The reads were then assembled via MEGAHIT (Li et al. 2016) (minimum contig length 1,500). Reads were backmapped to the assembly via Bowtie2 (Langmead et al. 2012, --very-sensitive-local). Bins produced by CONCOCT, MaxBin2, MetaBAT2, MetaBinner, and SemiBin2 were refined via DASTool, and quality was assessed via BUSCO. MAGs with SCG completeness ≥ 50% and SCG redundancy < 10% were kept. GTDB-Tk (Chaumeil et al. 2020) was used to assign taxonomy to MAGs, and these assignments were propagated to contigs. 

Metagenomic annotation and classification

Metagenomic contigs were annotated via Bakta (Schwengers et al. 2021) (--meta for the Pyrodigal module), and the identified CDS regions were functionally annotated via KofamScan (Aramaki et al. 2020) (used KO-specific adaptive score thresholds to keep significant hits). Contigs left unclassified after MAG classification propagation were classified using CAT (FAB von Meijenfeld et al. 2019) (-r 7 --diamond_mode sensitive). 

Ribo-ITP preprocessing and alignment

Ribo-ITP data were adapter-trimmed and quality-filtered using Cutadapt (Martin et al. 2011) (-a AAAAAAAAAACAAAAAAAAAA --overlap=4 --trimmed-only --maximum-length=56 --minimum-length=31 --quality-cutoff=28), and 12-nt UMIs were extracted from the 5’ end via UMI-tools (Smith et al. 2017), leaving reads between 15 and 40 nt. Reads were aligned to contigs using Bowtie2, permitting only exact full-length alignments (--end-to-end --score-min L,0,0), and deduplicated via UMI-tools (directional method). Multi-mappers were discarded. Alignments with ≥1 bp overlap with Bakta-annotated rRNA, tRNA, or tmRNA regions were removed via BEDTools and SAMTools (Quinlan & Hall, 2010; Li et al., 2009).

Coverage analysis

All analyses were performed in Python, with visualizations generated using Matplotlib. Ribo-ITP counts for predicted coding sequences were quantified using the pysam package. Reads were assigned to the gene that contained the largest overlap with the read. For the metagene analysis, 5’ ends were chosen as the anchor, as poly-A trimming made the 3’ end less reliable. For each gene, per-position counts in the stop- and start-metagene were divided by the total number of mapped 5’ ends aligned to that gene, and the resulting normalized profiles were summed across genes. 

Functional analysis

To taxonomically classify Ribo-ITP and metagenomic reads, contig classifications were transferred to their aligned reads. Reads aligned to unclassified contigs were used in gene-level analyses, but excluded from taxonomic analyses. To control for metagenomic abundance and gene length, Ribo-ITP RPKM values were divided by metagenomic coverage (computed as mean per-base coverage depth) for each gene. Then, the metagenome-normalized values were binned by KEGG BRITE C classifications; genes assigned to multiple KOs contributed no more than one count to each classification. 

To create the null distribution of function counts, genes were modeled as a multinomial distribution with probabilities proportional to gene length times metagenomic coverage. 10,000 null samples were generated, with the number of draws in each equal to the total number of Ribo-ITP reads aligned to genes. KEGG BRITE C classification counts were calculated in the same way as above. Fold-changes were calculated by dividing the metagenome-normalized value by the mean value of the null samples, and null standard deviation was calculated using the null distribution of means. P-values were estimated from the quantile of the observed data in the empirical distributions, FDR-corrected using the Benjamini-Hochberg method.
